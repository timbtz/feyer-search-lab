01 / THE CHALLENGE

A faster answer has to include the gradient.

Differometor models light moving through optical instruments, including gravitational-wave detectors. Feyer asked us to reduce the complete evaluation time while preserving the physical outputs and gradients. Compilation counts. Every frequency and design variable stays in the workload.

02 / THE SOLUTION

Skip the zeros.
Rethink compilation.

The example matrix has just 2,533 nonzero entries out of 564,001. Sparse assembly and SuperLU remove dense work. Then a host engine executes the JAX-traced component recipe with NumPy, sparse solves, and adjoint gradients.

The formulas remain the source of the physics. Later work reduces setup dispatch, small compilations, and unnecessary derivative work.

05 / TRUST THE EVIDENCE

PASS was the beginning.

Official large and aLIGO benchmarks, saved-array comparisons, a 14-case regression suite, and source review shaped the result. Review led to an explicit-adjoint repair in an experimental branch.

In the final six-setting SuperLU sweep, all settings passed the official checks. Only the default passed the extra seed-relative suite. We kept the repaired starting candidate.

    What improved?

    Compare complete first-run time. A correct result is not automatically a faster one.

    Times are medians of recorded full runs where available. Single measurements need confirmation. Comparisons stay within the same hardware and validation cohort.

    How we judge an improvement

    Primary objective

    Minimize complete first-run time: imports and setup, compilation, forward simulation, sensitivity, gradients, and result transfer.

    Useful tradeoffs

    Compilation, execution time, and peak memory also form a Pareto comparison. A gain in one can come with a cost in another. Exact Pareto membership and improvement beyond measurement noise are recorded separately.

    Correctness first

    Screening is provisional. Full acceptance requires the official benchmark and our separate saved-output and all-gradient comparison to pass.
